Preparation

In Chapters 7.1 and 7.2 we compute derivatives from measured values and from formulas. For this you need some math, Python and physics that you know from earlier semesters and chapters. Before the lecture, take the quiz on this page to check whether you are up to speed. It takes about ten minutes. What you should already know¶

From math you should be able to:

  • compute the slope of a straight line through two points

  • understand the derivative as a limit: the secant becomes the tangent

  • differentiate power functions with the power rule

  • work with powers of ten

In Python you should be able to:

  • write a for loop with range() and the index k, accessing values[k + 1]

  • create an array with np.zeros() and fill it in a loop

  • write functions with parameters and return, and use the index -1 and len()

  • find the index of the smallest or largest value with np.argmin() and np.argmax()

  • draw several plots one above the other with plt.subplots()

From physics you should know the following and be able to use it:

  • distance, velocity and acceleration with their units

  • When a vehicle drives straight ahead in one direction, its velocity is the derivative of the distance with respect to time, and its acceleration is the derivative of the velocity.

  • uniformly accelerated motion from rest

  • converting between m/s and km/h

Our application example, the AGV, is familiar from Chapter 6. You do not need to know anything else about automated guided vehicles.

Math¶

Math refresher: difference quotient, derivative, powers of ten

The slope of the secant through the points (x1,y1)(x_1, y_1)(x1​,y1​) and (x2,y2)(x_2, y_2)(x2​,y2​) is the difference quotient

m=y2−y1x2−x1.m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}.m=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​.

If the second point moves closer and closer to the first, the secant becomes the tangent. Its slope is the derivative:

f′(x)=lim⁡Δx→0f(x+Δx)−f(x)Δx.f'(x) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x + \Delta x) - f(x)}{\Delta x}.f′(x)=Δx→0lim​Δxf(x+Δx)−f(x)​.

By the power rule, the derivative of xnx^nxn is n xn−1n\,x^{n-1}nxn−1. For example, the derivative of 0.2 t20.2\,t^20.2t2 is 0.4 t0.4\,t0.4t. You can find the derivatives of other functions in the table of derivatives.

Physics¶

Physics refresher: distance, velocity, acceleration

When a vehicle drives straight ahead in one direction, its velocity vvv is the derivative of the distance sss traveled with respect to time, and its acceleration aaa is the derivative of the velocity:

v(t)=s′(t),a(t)=v′(t)=s′′(t).v(t) = s'(t), \qquad a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t).v(t)=s′(t),a(t)=v′(t)=s′′(t).

We measure distances in m, velocities in m/s and accelerations in m/s2\text{m/s}^2m/s2. For the conversion, 1 m/s=3.6 km/h1\,\text{m/s} = 3.6\,\text{km/h}1m/s=3.6km/h.

If a vehicle accelerates uniformly from rest at aaa, then after the time ttt

v=a t,s=a2 t2.v = a\,t, \qquad s = \frac{a}{2}\,t^2.v=at,s=2a​t2.

An AGV that accelerates from rest at 0.4 m/s20.4\,\text{m/s}^20.4m/s2 for 2 s2\,\text{s}2s is then moving at 0.8 m/s0.8\,\text{m/s}0.8m/s and has covered 0.8 m0.8\,\text{m}0.8m.
