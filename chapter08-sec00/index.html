Physics¶

Physics refresher: characteristic curve and cooling

Many sensors have a linear characteristic curve U=m⋅F+bU = m \cdot F + bU=m⋅F+b. The slope mmm is called the sensitivity, and the intercept bbb the zero offset. To get the force from a measured voltage, we solve for FFF:

F=U−bm.F = \frac{U - b}{m}.F=mU−b​.

The larger the temperature difference, the more heat a hot part gives off to its surroundings. That is why its temperature drops quickly at first and then more and more slowly. If the ambient temperature is constant and there is no additional cooling, the part’s temperature approaches the ambient temperature but never drops below it. You will learn the law behind this in Chapter 8.2.
