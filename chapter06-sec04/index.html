6.4 Turning into an Aisle

In Chapter 6.3 we plotted the AGV along a path. Now it turns into an aisle between racks, and we check whether the curve leaves enough clearance to the racks. Work through the parts in pairs if possible, and in order.

Project: Does the AGV clear the rack corner? (✩✩)

The AGV is supposed to fetch a box from the aisle between rack 2 and rack 3. It drives to the right in the main aisle and turns left into the aisle on a quarter circle with a radius of 2 m2\,\text{m}2m. # functions from Chapter 5.3
def transformation_matrix(angle, tx, ty):
    """Homogeneous matrix: rotate by angle (radians), then shift by (tx, ty)."""
    return np.array([
        [np.cos(angle), -np.sin(angle), tx],
        [np.sin(angle),  np.cos(angle), ty],
        [0.0,            0.0,           1.0],
    ])

def transform(T, points):
    """Applies the homogeneous matrix T to all points, row = [x, y]."""
    points_hom = np.ones((len(points), 3))
    points_hom[:, 0:2] = points
    result = (T @ points_hom.T).T
    return result[:, 0:2]

However, the angles there are 5°5°5°, 15°15°15° up to 85°85°85°, and not 0°0°0°, 10°10°10° up to 80°80°80° as the tangents at these points would be. As in Chapter 6.3, psi[k] is the direction of the secant to the next path point. At the start of the quarter circle, the tangent is horizontal, but the secant to the next point on the circle already rises. On a circle, its direction lies exactly halfway between the tangents at its two ends, i.e. at 5°5°5°. On the circular segments, the AGV is therefore half an angular step ahead of the path. At path point 13 at the end of the quarter circle, on the other hand, the secant already runs along the straight line in the aisle. There it coincides with the tangent, and the AGV points exactly in the direction of the path at 90°90°90°.

Bonus exercise: Dead reckoning in the curve (✩✩✩)

How does the AGV’s controller know its pose while driving? Our AGV has a differential drive: its two drive wheels on the left and right are driven independently, and the controller counts the revolutions of each wheel. From these counts, it computes after each segment of travel how far the AGV has moved and by how much it has turned, from the vehicle’s own point of view. This method is called dead reckoning.

On the quarter circle, the wheel measurement reports the same segment nine times: a circular arc with radius R=2 mR = 2\,\text{m}R=2m on which the AGV turns by Δψ=10°\Delta\psi = 10°Δψ=10°. In the vehicle coordinate system before the segment, the center of the circle is at (0,R)(0, R)(0,R). The AGV therefore ends up at (Rsin⁡Δψ, R (1−cos⁡Δψ))(R \sin\Delta\psi,\ R\,(1 - \cos\Delta\psi))(RsinΔψ, R(1−cosΔψ)) and is rotated by Δψ\Delta\psiΔψ. Like the position of the scanner in Chapter 6.2, this describes a new vehicle coordinate system relative to the old one: T_step = transformation_matrix(dpsi, radius * np.sin(dpsi), radius * (1 - np.cos(dpsi))). The controller therefore gets the new pose the same way as TWS\mathbf{T}_{WS}TWS​ in Chapter 6.2, by multiplying from the right:

TWV,new=TWV,old⋅Tstep.\mathbf{T}_{WV,\text{new}} = \mathbf{T}_{WV,\text{old}} \cdot \mathbf{T}_{\text{step}}.TWV,new​=TWV,old​⋅Tstep​.
