6.2 Safety Scanner and Protective Field

In Chapter 6.1 the scanner sat at the center of the vehicle. Now a second scanner is added at the front of the vehicle. It makes sure that the AGV stops in time. Work through the parts in pairs if possible, and in order.

Project: Does the AGV have to stop? (✩✩)

The AGV from Chapter 6.1 is at (4,2)(4, 2)(4,2) with ψ=30°\psi = 30°ψ=30°. A safety scanner sits in the middle of its front edge. # functions from Chapter 5.3
def transformation_matrix(angle, tx, ty):
    """Homogeneous matrix: rotate by angle (radians), then shift by (tx, ty)."""
    return np.array([
        [np.cos(angle), -np.sin(angle), tx],
        [np.sin(angle),  np.cos(angle), ty],
        [0.0,            0.0,           1.0],
    ])

def transform(T, points):
    """Applies the homogeneous matrix T to all points, row = [x, y]."""
    points_hom = np.ones((len(points), 3))
    points_hom[:, 0:2] = points
    result = (T @ points_hom.T).T
    return result[:, 0:2]

Closing question

  1. Why do we check the protective field in vehicle coordinates and not in warehouse coordinates? Which of the matrices TVS\mathbf{T}_{VS}TVS​ and TWV\mathbf{T}_{WV}TWV​ does the check need, and what follows if the controller has the wrong pose for the vehicle?

  2. Two neighboring beams are 5°5°5° apart. How far apart are their scan points at a distance of 1 m1\,\text{m}1m? Would the scanner reliably detect a chair leg with a diameter of 4 cm4\,\text{cm}4cm at this distance?

Solution closing question

  1. In the vehicle coordinate system, the protective field always has the same boundaries, no matter where the vehicle is and where it points. Its edges are parallel to the axes, so four simple comparisons are enough, as for the lid in the box in Chapter 5.2. In warehouse coordinates, the protective field is rotated by ψ\psiψ, its edges are oblique, and it is somewhere else for every new pose. For the check in vehicle coordinates, we only need TVS\mathbf{T}_{VS}TVS​, i.e. the fixed position of the scanner on the vehicle. We do not need TWV\mathbf{T}_{WV}TWV​, and thus the pose, for this. In our simplified model, the check is therefore independent of the pose in the warehouse, and a wrong pose does not change the decision whether the AGV stops. This is why real safety scanners evaluate their protective field themselves, independently of the vehicle’s navigation. That alone does not guarantee that the AGV actually comes to a stop in time, though. For that, the scanner and its mounting, the size of the protective field, the speed and braking distance, the response time and the handling of faults, among other things, must all be designed to meet the safety requirements.

  2. Neighboring beams are 5°5°5° apart, which is 5⋅π/180≈0.0875 \cdot \pi / 180 \approx 0.0875⋅π/180≈0.087 in radians. As with the circular arc in Chapter 5.2, their scan points at a distance of 1 m1\,\text{m}1m are therefore about 1 m⋅0.087=8.7 cm1\,\text{m} \cdot 0.087 = 8.7\,\text{cm}1m⋅0.087=8.7cm apart. A chair leg with a diameter of 4 cm4\,\text{cm}4cm can stand exactly between two beams and is then not detected. The farther away an object is, the larger the gaps become. Real safety scanners therefore have a much finer angular resolution. Which objects they reliably detect also depends on the diameter of the laser beam, on the reflection and on evaluating several consecutive scans. Our scan with 5°5°5° is only meant as an overview.

Bonus exercise: Determining the yaw angle with a reflector (✩✩✩)

The AGV’s controller continuously computes the pose from the rotations of the wheels. Small measurement errors add up in the process, especially for the yaw angle. To correct this, a reflector is mounted on the pillar, at (6.3,0.7)(6.3, 0.7)(6.3,0.7) according to the warehouse map. For localization, the controller also evaluates the measurement data of the front scanner, with an angular resolution of 1°1°1° instead of the 5°5°5° of our protective field scan. In practice, a separate navigation scanner often takes over this task, because not every safety scanner provides such data. The evaluation recognizes the reflector by its particularly bright echo. It is at the distance r=2.39 mr = 2.39\,\text{m}r=2.39m and the angle φ=−72°\varphi = -72°φ=−72°, both values rounded. At any other yaw angle, the reflector would be somewhere else in the warehouse, at 210°210°210° even more than 5 m5\,\text{m}5m away from its position on the map. A function whose smallest value gives the quantity we are looking for is called a cost function. If the position is also unknown, we are looking for three quantities xxx, yyy and ψ\psiψ. Two distinguishable and correctly assigned reflectors determine the pose uniquely.
